How it started

Bananas hanging in the compound, and hunger in the house

After distributing many banana suckers across various areas of Kiambu while serving as the Women’s Representative, and after the trees matured and began fruiting, the subsequent banana-fruit glut brought low prices and waste. It became apparent that fruit alone could not bring the economic empowerment that had been envisaged.

Visiting homes and witnessing hunger, and lack of basics like sugar, yet bunches of bananas hung about in the compound, made me embark on a journey of more meaningful value addition , one that would not only address the banana glut and post-harvest losses, but also the utilisation of the entire banana plant.

That is the through-line of everything here: take what the land already produces in surplus, and add enough value to it that a farming family is better off.

Bansoko Centre

The whole plant, not just the fruit

At Bansoko Centre we promote utilisation of the entire banana plant. We provide machinery to extract banana fibre from banana stems. The fibre is then dried and dyed as necessary.

Some fibre is baled and packed into 15kg bales for onward processing into finer fabrics. The extraction and weaving process requires very low power consumption.

Artisans are engaged to produce various banana-fibre artifacts, and to produce and supply eco-friendly bags and packaging material , including the woven carriers our wine ships in.
