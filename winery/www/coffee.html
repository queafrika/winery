The farm

Njagũ, on a hillside above a permanent stream

Our specialty coffee farm sits in Njagũ village, near the boundary of the Lari and Githunguri sub-counties of Kiambu. It lies on a hilly slope ranging from 1,550 to 1,725 metres above sea level, and borders a permanent stream at its lower end.

The area receives an annual average rainfall of 1,380mm, and temperatures range between 15 °C and 23 °C. Its soils are reddish to brown, deep, well-drained fertile humic nitisols derived from volcanic rock , the combination that gives Kenyan arabica its reputation.

History

Trees that have been yielding for seventy years

  1. 1912

    Coffee growing is introduced to the wider Kiambu area by Christian missionaries, and subsequently adopted by white-settler farmers between 1912 and 1950 , using both forced and wage labour, mainly by women and children.

  2. 1954

    For decades, colonial rule prohibited native Africans from growing cash crops like tea and coffee. That prohibition holds until 1954.

  3. 1990s

    A more disease-resistant variety, Ruiru 11 from the Kenya Agricultural Research Laboratories, begins taking root from the early 1990s.

  4. 2000s

    The Kenyan coffee sector goes through turmoil and low farmer prices in the late 1990s and early 2000s. A number of farms are neglected, or their crop uprooted altogether.

  5. 2023

    We acquired one such farm and have been on a rehabilitation journey since: drilling a borehole, trenching and benching, adding a new drip irrigation system, and replacing gaps with improved varieties.

In honour of the women who planted it

In honour of the women and children then enlisted into forced labour establishing these coffee farms, part of our coffee proceeds goes to support aged women welfare programmes in the local communities.

Processing

Washed, then fourteen days in the sun

Our coffee is wet-processed , washed. The skin and pulp are removed from the cherry before the bean is set out to sun-dry for up to 14 days, until the moisture content goes below 11%.

Sometimes we send our coffee to the local cooperative society factory, where it undergoes the same washing, pulping and sun-drying.

How we farm

Good agricultural practices, in practice

We believe in and practise the following on every block of the farm.

{{ icon("leaf", 22) }}

Soil first

Soil conservation and minimal tillage, with trenching and benching across the slope to hold water and stop erosion.

{{ icon("flask", 22) }}

Minimal spray

Minimal spraying, using approved organics, and application of organic manure rather than routine chemical inputs.

{{ icon("shield", 22) }}

Resistant replanting

Crop gaps replaced with disease-resistant varieties, alongside shade regulation and selective harvesting of ripe cherry only.

Export

TerraNova Coffee LLC, United States

TerraNova Coffee LLC is a USA-registered company affiliated to Ropen Coffee & Fine Foods Limited, through which we supply Kenyan specialty coffee into the American market.

  • Contracting estate farms and guiding them on the good agricultural practices the specialty market requires
  • Ensuring a steady supply of single-origin, traceable specialty coffee
  • Providing a guaranteed market for contracted farmers
  • Practising fair trade, and continuing the welfare of the aged women who planted coffee in the Kenyan highlands from the 1950s
